# Joinery 0.5.0

Not released yet. Changes since [0.4.0](0.4.0.md).

## Share your game

- **Share**, a new tab beside Timeline, builds the game for the web, Windows,
  macOS and Linux with stock Godot 4.7 (`joinery share build`). Joinery keeps
  an export preset per target, zips each build with a README and licence
  notes, checks sizes against itch.io's limits, starts the build for your own
  computer once and lets the playtester play it. Export templates are pinned
  in `policy/export_templates.toml` and downloaded only when you press
  **Download and build**.
- **Store media** (`joinery share media`): 4 to 8 screenshots of different
  moments from a seeded run, a 30-second trailer recorded with Godot's Movie
  Maker and encoded in the offline worker, and a 630 × 500 cover composed in
  Krita. All from the game itself, with recipes in `share/recipes/`.
- **Page text** (`joinery share page`): an itch.io page drafted from the brief,
  the controls and the credits, saved to `share/page.md` for you to edit.
- **Publish to itch.io** (`joinery share itch`): pushes the builds with butler,
  itch.io's upload tool, using your own login. Only you can publish; agents
  are refused. butler is installed only with your consent, pinned in
  `policy/butler.toml` (ADR 0046).

## Make everything match

- **`/match`**, or **Make it match** in Settings, on the Review tab's art
  bible row and in the art direction, checks the whole project against the
  approved art bible and fixes what drifts (ADR 0045). The **Match** card
  shows one headline, a score per area (**Palette 72% · Models 100% · Sound
  90%**) and the most serious findings with what was measured and what the
  bible asks. `joinery match --project <path>` prints the same audit
  (`--json`, `--area`, `--plan`); no model is needed.
- It measures palette distance (CIEDE2000), texture sizes and texel density,
  model scale, pivots and budgets, material roughness and metallic, sound
  loudness and true peak, and two new optional tables in the bible's checked
  rules: `[environment]` (tonemap, exposure, fog, glow and colour grading of
  every Environment) and `[ui]` (allowed fonts, theme colours on the palette).
- **Fix all** (or **Fix** on one area) makes the fixes in the app that owns
  each file, as small reviewed changes running side by side: a palette-map
  layer or a resize in Krita (`edit_image`), apply scale, pivot, decimate or
  texel density in Blender (`apply_scale`, `set_pivot`, `reduce_triangles`,
  `set_texel_density`), material values, loudness on the rendered sound, and
  the Environment's or theme's values through Godot. Each change is checked,
  read by the reviewer and decided by you; after an accept the scores rise on
  the card.
- `[match] exclude` and `[match] final` in `joinery.toml` (or `joinery match
  --final <file>`) keep files exactly as they are.

## Parametric kits

- **Kits** (ADR 0044): rocks, crates, barrels, fences, simple buildings,
  low-poly trees and bushes, made in Blender by hand-written procedural code
  in `kits/blender/` (MIT) from typed parameters and a seed. No model is
  involved; the same values make the same model.
- Kits follow the approved art bible on their own: palette colours, roughness
  and metallic in range, the folder's triangle budget and pivot, scale 1. Each
  model is a `.blend` under `art/sources/kits/` and a GLB (with LOD GLBs when
  asked), recorded in `joinery.lock` with a recipe that `joinery rebuild`
  replays byte for byte.
- Agents get `kit_list`, `kit_make`, `kit_batch` ("50 rocks, mossier,
  smaller" in one Blender run), `kit_edit` (change a made set, names kept)
  and `scatter`, which places models on a surface in a saved scene, spaced
  and turned at random, clear of a path or beside it.
- The asset workspace has a **Kits** tab: sliders for a kit's parameters, a
  preview Blender draws a moment after each change, **Make** and **Make 10
  variations**. A kit-made model names its kit, and **Kit settings** changes
  its set. Both make a change waiting for your review. Also on the command
  line: `joinery kits list|preview|make|edit`.
- `joinery rebuild` signs a recipe's `.blend` files in one Blender run.

## Shared kits

- **Export kit…** and **Import kit…** in the Kits tab (ADR 0051) move models,
  sounds, materials, prefab scenes and kit generators with presets from one
  project to another as a `.joinerykit` file you carry yourself, with their
  recipes, origins and licences. `joinery kit export|import|remove|list` on the
  command line; `--plan` shows what would happen.
- Export brings what your choice needs to be made again and refuses what Rule
  Zero doesn't allow to travel: reference images, files without a
  `joinery.lock` entry and game code. Custom-tool assets go only when you tick
  the box, and stay marked.
- Import checks every file against the kit's SHA-256 list and refuses links,
  `..` paths, executables and oversized or inflating files. It is one reviewed
  change under `kits/<name>/`: paths in recipes and scenes are moved to match,
  each asset gets a `kit` entry in `joinery.lock` (kit, version, author,
  licence, how it was made), and `joinery rebuild` makes the kit's assets again
  to the same fingerprints. Same names from different kits get their own
  folder; importing a newer version updates in place and shows what changed;
  a kit a scene still uses can't be removed.
- Imported generators join the Kits list and `kit_list` with their presets, so
  Journeyman makes models with them; agents can't import, export or remove a
  kit. The Share build's licence notes and the page credits name every kit.

## Variants side by side

- `/variants N <request>` (2 to 4 ways, 3 by default), **Try 3 ways** in the
  composer's options menu and **Try other ways** on a change waiting for review
  make the request a few different ways at once, one agent per direction
  ("chunky and worn", "slim and clean"). The lead agent offers it when you're
  unsure and starts it with `start_variants` only when you agree (ADR 0042).
- The ways show side by side in one strip, in the chat and above a way's review:
  pictures, sounds to play, changed lines, each way's direction and facts.
  **Use this** keeps one way as one Timeline entry and discards the others at
  once; **Mix…** starts one more way from notes on these; **Discard all** ends
  it.
- Ways of one request may change the same files (they are alternatives); every
  way is checked and reviewed like any change, counts towards `[agents]
  parallel`, and waits its turn when the limit is reached. Saved chats record
  the ways and the pick.

## Sketch to blockout

- Attach a drawing of a level (or a top-down screenshot) and send `/blockout`, or
  ask Journeyman to "build this level". It reads the sketch into a typed layout
  (rooms, walls with doors and windows, corridors, stairs, platforms, spawn, goals,
  coins, pickups, checkpoints, hazards), Joinery checks it, and builds a greybox scene
  with collision: CSG in 3D, solid rectangles in 2D, a named node for each piece
  (ADR 0043).
- Before anything is built, Joinery checks the spawn reaches every goal with your
  starting point's own player, and that doors, corridors and stairs fit it. Problems
  go back to the agent to fix.
- After building, the playtest bot walks the staged level in Godot from the spawn to
  every goal; the review leads with "Walkable: spawn reaches 3 of 3 goals" and shows
  your sketch beside the plan.
- The layout is kept in `design/blockouts/<name>.toml` as the level's recipe: ask for
  changes ("move the door to the north wall") and the level is rebuilt from it, the
  same bytes every time. `joinery blockout check|build|walk` does the same from the
  command line.
- `joinery playtest --route` walks a planned route instead of exploring.

## Animation pass

- **Animations in the Blender tab**: a model's actions are listed under its
  preview with length, loop and frame rate, and play on the model with play
  and pause, scrubbing, loop, ping-pong or once, speeds from 0.25× to 2× and
  ← / → to go through them. Click one, or **Use in message**, to target it
  ("make the run bouncier" changes only the run).
- **Motions made in Blender** (`make_cycle`): walk, run, idle, jump (start, air,
  land), attack and wave, as Joinery's own keyframes on Rigify and MPFB2 rigs
  or a hand-made humanoid, with planted feet and loops that meet themselves.
  Speed, length, stride, bounce, arm swing and lean are parameters.
- **Action edits** (`edit_action`): retime, amplitude per bone group, bounce,
  offset, foot lock, loop seam, mirror, rename, copy and delete.
- **Animation pass** (`audit_animations`, `animation_pass`): compares a
  character's actions with the clips the game plays and makes the missing ones.
- **Review clips**: an animation change shows before and after as two looping
  clips rendered by Blender in the worker, with facts like "Run: 0.8 s loop,
  bounce +30%".
- **Players use them**: the third-person and first-person blueprints' players
  play idle, walk, run and jump clips when the character model has them
  (ADR 0047).

## Steam before you have an app ID

The Steam app ID is optional, so you can plan a Steam release before there is a
Steamworks page. The wizard field now reads "Steam app ID (optional)", and
Steam stays off in the game until an ID is set. `joinery steam --project <path>
--app-id <digits>` adds or changes it later (`--app-id 0` clears it, no flags
shows what is set), so Project Settings is no longer the only place to set it.

## Fixed

- A Blender edit of a model whose scale wasn't applied failed its own checks:
  the copy of the old document the recipe keeps was judged as an asset.
  Nothing under `recipes/` is judged any more.
- Two parallel changes that each made a source folder clashed on its empty
  `.gdignore`; those markers are no longer claimed.
- The reviewer couldn't open a file the change made ("File does not exist:
  res://game/keep_blockout.tscn") and sent the change back for nothing: the Godot
  tools took a `res://` file path as a folder named `res:`. Every agent's Godot
  reads and writes now accept `res://` paths. The reviewer, debugger and other
  read-only agents read the change under review through a read-only view of its
  stage, new and changed files included, and no agent can read another task's
  staged change.
- Inspecting a Blender model named by its `res://` path failed
  ("Cannot read file /work/res:/art/…"); it takes either form now.
- After the reviewer sent a blockout back and it was built again, the review
  lost the sketch beside the plan. A resubmitted blockout keeps the sketch it
  first showed, however the agent names it the second time.
- A read-only agent's own shell and file tools (`cat`, `sed`, Read, read_file…)
  could open any staged change's folder. Each agent session now carries the one
  change it may read (the change under review, for the reviewer), and the shared
  policy hook refuses any other `.joinery/transactions/` folder, by path, glob or
  `cd`, for Codex, Claude Code, Gemini CLI and opencode; read-only agents can't
  write there at all.
- Review pictures showed small in a large empty frame. The picture frame now
  grows with the panel, pictures fill it at their shape (a sketch and its plan
  share the width), and pixel art and small pictures are enlarged crisp while
  photos stay smooth; the filmstrip and the variant tiles too. A model's renders
  and scene snapshots no longer sit as dark boxes between empty bands: the frame
  takes their own background.
- Slider knobs were hard to see in the light theme. Slider tracks and knobs now
  have 3:1 contrast on every surface in both themes.
- Clicking the **As made** way's picture in a request tried other ways didn't
  open its review; it opens the main review, under the ways.
