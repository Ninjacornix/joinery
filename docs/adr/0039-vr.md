# VR

Accepted: 2026-11-11. Scope: the `vr` feature (`features/vr`), the `vr-room`
blueprint, the `vr-3d` bible preset, and an optional `unsupported` sentence
on features (`joinery/blueprints.py`).

Users asked for VR: a feature that turns a 3D starting point into a headset
game, and a small room to start a VR game from.

**OpenXR is Godot's, nothing is downloaded.** Godot 4.7 ships OpenXR. The
feature sets `xr/openxr/enabled`, `xr/shaders/enabled`, turns off
`xr/openxr/startup_alert` and points `xr/openxr/default_action_map` at
`res://features/vr/openxr_action_map.tres`, the action map stock Godot writes
for a new OpenXR project, kept as a plain Godot resource. No loader, toolkit
(GodotXR Tools or similar) or vendor plugin is added; the rig, hands, pickup
contract, locomotion and menu panel are ours, small and MIT like the rest of
the game-facing code. The OpenXR runtime itself (SteamVR, Meta, Monado…) is
the player's, as a graphics driver is.

**One rig for every 3D player.** The `VR` autoload looks at Godot's OpenXR
interface. When it is initialised, the root viewport renders to the headset
and an `XROrigin3D` rig (`xr_rig.tscn`: camera, two `XRController3D` hands
with greybox meshes, a pointer ray per hand) is added to the local player of
every level. The rig stops the player's own physics and mouse look and moves
its body itself, so first-person, third-person (seen from the character's
eyes) and the VR room share it with no per-blueprint adapter:

- the body follows the head when the player walks in the room;
- the move stick walks at 1–3 m/s relative to where the head looks;
- the other stick snaps 30° or 45° about the head, and pushed forward aims a
  teleport arc that lands only on floor no steeper than 30°, within 8 m and
  not on anything in group `no_teleport`;
- a vignette closes in while moving or turning (strength is a setting);
- seated play lifts the rig 0.45 m; left-handed swaps the sticks and the
  pointer hand;
- grip grabs the nearest node in group `pickable` within 0.2 m (it must have
  `grab(holder)` and `release(velocity)`) and throws it with the hand's speed;
- the trigger uses an `interactable` the pointer is on (`use`, or `interact`
  for third-person props) through `Game.request_action`, so online play sees
  it like any other action;
- the core `Menus` layer draws into a panel 1.5 m in front of the player
  (`CanvasLayer.custom_viewport`), sized like the window so menus keep their
  layout; the pointer clicks it and the menu button opens the pause menu.

Settings live in the player's settings file (section `vr`) and on a **VR
comfort** settings page. With the controller feature, hand rumble and the
stick deadzone follow its Controls page; with save-load, a save also keeps
which way the rig was turned (comfort settings are never part of a save).

**The desktop is the fallback, never a failure.** Without an OpenXR runtime
Godot logs that OpenXR could not start and starts in normal mode (the
start-up alert is off, so nothing blocks); the autoload sees an uninitialised
interface, carries no rig and shows "No VR headset found: playing on the
desktop." once. Every blueprint's own mouse, keyboard and pad controls stay.
The VR room is a complete desktop game too: look, walk, pick up and carry
with interact, press the button.

**VR needs 3D.** The feature lists the 3D starting points. A feature may now
say why it is refused (`unsupported` in `feature.toml`); the sentence follows
"VR does not work with the 2D platformer blueprint." in the CLI and the
wizard.

**The VR room.** A greybox room 8 m square: a button on a post opens a glass
case, the cube inside goes on the table to win, and a ball and a can are
there to throw. Tables, posts, walls and the shelf are `no_teleport`; the
floor is not. It ships its brief, a `vr-3d` bible preset (head-locked camera
rules, true scale, reach heights, 72/90 fps and triangle and draw-call
budgets for standalone and PC headsets), a card screenshot made by Godot
(the desktop view, with the notice), and adapters for save-load, online play
(the button and the win replicate; held objects stay in each player's own
game) and translations.

**What was tested, and what was not.** No headset or OpenXR runtime was
available while this was built. Tests run stock Godot headless:

- the comfort maths, the rig scene, grab and throw, the comfort page and
  settings kept between sessions;
- the real no-runtime start (OpenXR on, Godot's own error, the desktop
  notice, no other error), and the editor opening the project cleanly;
- `-- --vr-simulate` carries the rig without a headset, on all three 3D
  starting points: walking, snap turns, teleport onto the floor and not onto
  tables, the pointer using a switch, lever or button, a hand grabbing a room
  object, the menu panel opening and its Resume clicked by the pointer, and a
  saved turn coming back on load;
- other test runs pass `--xr-mode off` to projects with OpenXR on, which the
  feature treats as the desktop.

What only a headset shows is untested: tracking poses driving the camera and
hands, the action map's bindings on real controllers, haptics, the refresh
rate taken from the runtime, how the vignette and the menu panel look in
each eye, and comfort itself. The first person with a headset should try the
VR room before anyone relies on it.
